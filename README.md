# Cairo Walks

A walking guide (English, Arabic, French and Spanish) to historic Cairo. Pick a route, browse the monuments, cafés, workshops and meeting points along it, and listen to an audio guide while you stand in front of each place.

**Live:** [cairowalks.vercel.app](https://cairowalks.vercel.app)

## Features

- **Five curated walking routes**: Ahl al-Bayt, Al-Muizz Street, Coptic Cairo, Al-Darb Al-Ahmar & the Citadel, and Fustat, Roda & Old Cairo, each with distance, walking time and highlights. Distance and walking time are stored as numbers (`distanceKm`, `hoursMin`, `hoursMax`) and written in the visitor's language ("2,5 km · 2 à 3 heures", "2.5 كم · 2–3 ساعات"). Older content with text such as "2-3 hours" is read as numbers, and the English text is still stored next to the numbers for app versions that read it. See `src/lib/route-format.ts`.
- **Help me choose a walk**: under the routes, three quick questions (about 1 hour, 2 to 3 hours or half a day; mosques and shrines, museums and old houses, food and cafés, crafts; and whether step-free access is needed, which puts the routes with more step-free stops first) suggest the best route, and a close second, with why ("2–3 hours · 9 cafés and restaurants") and **Show this walk**. Routes are ranked from their walking time and what their places are, so it works offline in every language. See `src/lib/route-finder.ts`.
- **List and map views**: filter places by category (monuments, coffee shops, restaurants, workshops, stationery & toys, meeting points) or search by name. The map shows the route line with arrows for the walking direction, the stops numbered in walking order (as "Stop 3 of 20" in the list, with a gold check once visited; never clustered, so the walk reads 1, 2, 3 at any zoom), the other places clustered, and your current location, with a modern and an antique style.
- **Immersive audio guide**: Gemini rewrites each place's description as a short narrated story and voices it. If AI audio isn't available, the browser's built-in voice reads it instead. **Read the transcript** under the player shows the words of the narration, in the guide's language, for visitors who can't listen (deaf or hard of hearing, no headphones); it's saved with offline routes. The script is stored in Blob next to the recording (same key, `.txt`) and sent with the audio in the `X-Guide-Transcript` header, so the two never drift apart in any cache. Guides made before transcripts have no toggle until they're generated again in the admin.
  Guides play through one shared `<audio>` element (`src/lib/guide-player.ts`), so only one plays at a time and phones show it on the lock screen and in notifications (Media Session): the place's name, its route and its photo, with play, pause, stop and 10-second skip buttons. In walk mode the lock screen's next and previous buttons move between stops, and the new stop's guide plays on. A speed button next to a playing guide cycles 1×, 1.25×, 1.5× and 0.75×; the choice is remembered on the device and also applies to the browser's voice.
- **Live insights**: an on-demand, Google Search–grounded summary of current visitor info and reviews for a place, with source links.
- **Near me tab**: a third tab beside List and Map that lists places by distance (this route or all routes). Once it's on, location keeps running on every tab, with a small "Near me on · Stop" pill, and arriving within about 30 m of any place offers its audio guide with a Play button (audio never starts by itself).
- **Visited tracking**: mark places as visited and see each route's progress ("6 / 14 visited"). Visited places show a check on their card and on the map. Progress is saved in the browser, so it works offline and needs no account.
- **Saved places**: a bookmark on every place (its row, its full card and walk mode) saves it to find again, on any route. **Saved** beside the routes' heading shows how many and opens the list, grouped by route; tapping one switches to its route and opens it, and × removes it. Saved places carry a small gold bookmark on the map. Like visited places, they're kept on the device (`src/lib/id-set-store.ts`, shared by both), so it works offline with no account and stays in step across open tabs; a place deleted in the admin quietly drops out. **Share list** shares a link to them (`/?saved=12,31,40`, at most 50 places) through the phone's share sheet, or copies it; opening it shows "3 places shared with you", grouped by route, with **Save all** and a bookmark on each. Unknown or deleted places are skipped and nothing is sent to the server. See `savedShareUrl` in `src/lib/share.ts`. Under the saved places, **Recently viewed** lists the last 10 places whose card was opened, newest first, with their route, a tap to open each again, and **Clear**; kept on the device only (`src/lib/recent.ts`).
- **"Was this helpful?"**: the end of each place's card (and walk mode) asks **Yes / No**, once per place on the device. The vote goes with the anonymous usage counts (place and vote only, no visitor data), visitors with Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control aren't asked, and votes made with no connection are kept on the device and sent once it's back online (that now applies to all the counts). After **No**, an optional one-tap reason (information out of date, too short, hard to find, audio problem, something else, which points to Report a problem) goes with the counts the same way: place and reason only. The admin's Stats tab lists votes per place with the share of Yes, sorted by most No (or most votes), with the reasons under each place ("2 × out of date · 1 × audio problem"), each linking to edit the place. See `src/components/HelpfulPrompt.tsx` and `src/lib/stats.ts`.
- **Report a problem**: every place has a link to report a wrong location (drag a pin or send your GPS position), wrong text, a closure or a photo problem. Reports go to the admin dashboard and don't include any personal details.
- **English, Arabic, French and Spanish**, with full right-to-left layout for Arabic. The language picker in the header remembers the choice; the first visit follows the browser's language. French and Spanish content is optional: routes and places without it show their English (marked `lang="en"` for screen readers), and their audio is narrated in that language from the English description until there is a translated one. Interface strings live in `src/i18n/fr.ts` and `src/i18n/es.ts`, keyed by the English text; a test fails if a component string is missing from either.
  Every language is listed once, in `LANGUAGES` in `src/i18n/lang.ts` (its name, content-field suffix, locale, speech voice, link-preview locale and direction), and its interface dictionary in `DICTIONARIES`. Each dictionary is a separate file, downloaded only for visitors using that language: the starting language's before the first paint, another one before switching to it (and cached for offline use like the rest of the app). Content pickers, fallbacks, validation, the server's language checks, audio, the admin's fields and the language picker all read that list, so adding a language is mostly a new row, a dictionary, and the few sentences built around numbers (`byLang`) and in the opening-hours and route formatters.
- **Share a route**: a **Share route** button opens the phone's share sheet, or copies the link where there's none. Links like `/r/fustat-roda` open the app on that route (`/?route=fustat-roda` works too), and show the route's photo, name and description as a preview in WhatsApp, iMessage and social apps: `vercel.json` rewrites `/r/…` to `api/share.ts`, which adds the preview tags to the app's page. In the admin, each route has a **QR code** to download or print as a card in English and Arabic (make cards from the live site's admin, since the code uses its address).
- **Getting there**: under a route's buttons, where the walk starts (the first stop, or a meeting point the admin picks), **Directions** to it in the phone's maps app (by coordinates), the nearest metro station and the walk from it ("Nearest metro: Mar Girgis · 2 min walk") and an optional note. **Show the driver** opens a full-screen card in large Arabic, whatever the app's language ("من فضلك، أريد الذهاب إلى:" and the start's Arabic name and area), to show a taxi driver. The printed route sheet gives the start, with its Arabic name, and the metro. In the admin, the route editor has **Getting there**: the start point, the metro station in each language and its walking minutes, and the note; **Fill in missing** drafts their French and Spanish. See `src/lib/getting-there.ts` and `src/components/GettingThere.tsx`.
- **Printable route sheet**: **Print route sheet** (under the download button) opens the browser's print dialog with an A4 sheet in the visitor's language (right to left in Arabic): the route's name, distance, time and description, a schematic map of the numbered stops (drawn from their coordinates, so it prints crisp and needs no map tiles), each stop with its walk from the one before, its week's hours on one line ("Monday–Thursday 9 am–5 pm · Friday Closed") and one line about it, and a QR code that opens the route in the app. Once some stop's access is set, the sheet also gives the route's step-free summary and each stop's access. The sheet never shows on screen, and it and the QR code library load only when printing. See `src/components/RouteSheet.tsx` and `src/lib/route-sheet.ts`.
- **Share a place**: the share button on each place opens the phone's share sheet (or copies the link) with a `/p/<id>` link that always names the current language, e.g. `/p/8?lang=es` or `/p/8?lang=en` (so an English share opened on an Arabic phone stays in English). It opens the app on that place, and WhatsApp, iMessage and social apps show the place's photo, name (with its route) and description as the preview, in the link's language (French and Spanish fall back to English). The same `api/share.ts` handles it; unknown or deleted places go to the home page. A visitor who hasn't picked a language yet gets the link's. Older `/?loc=<id>` links still work.
- **Help**: the lifebuoy in the header opens a sheet that works offline, in every language: Egypt's emergency numbers to call with one tap (ambulance 123, tourist police 126, police 122, fire 180); **Show where I am**, with the position from the phone's GPS as coordinates to read out, a maps link, **Copy to send** and the nearest stop on the route; phrases to show someone in large Arabic ("I need help", "Please call an ambulance", "Where is the nearest toilet?", "Where can I find drinking water?", and "I'm lost" with the route's start); and practical tips (water and shade, dress for mosques, small change, taxi fares). From the admin's **Help** tab it can also show local contacts to call with one tap (a guide, a hotel), extra tips and extra phrases, in each language. The sheet loads only when opened. See `src/lib/help.ts`, `src/lib/help-content.ts` and `src/components/HelpSheet.tsx`.
- **Language and display**: the globe in the header opens one small sheet with the language, a **text size** (Standard, Large, Larger), a **Theme** (Auto, which follows the phone and switches with it, Light or Dark, for evening walks) and **High contrast**, for reading outdoors in bright sun. Larger text scales only the text sizes (not spacing), so nothing overflows at 360 px in any language; high contrast darkens the greys and gold text to at least 7:1 (WCAG AAA) and firms up borders. Both are remembered on the device, apply everywhere (walk mode and dialogs included), and high contrast starts on when the phone asks for more contrast. The dark theme turns the palette over (page, borders, greys, gold and green text) and darkens the few fixed light surfaces and tinted notes, keeping text at 4.5:1 or more (7:1 with high contrast); photos and map tiles stay as they are, and the admin stays light. See `src/lib/display.ts` and the `data-text` / `data-contrast` / `data-theme` rules in `src/index.css`.

Photos show a placeholder with **Show photo** until tapped (those already saved offline show as usual), audio guides aren't prepared in the background (only when played, and without the lock-screen photo), and the map works as before. It's on by default when the browser asks to save data (`Save-Data`), and the choice is remembered on the device. See `src/components/PlacePhoto.tsx` and `src/lib/display.ts`.
- **Place list**: each route's places are compact rows (photo, name, "Stop 3 of 20" or the category, visited and opening status) in two groups: the **stops on the walk**, in walking order, then the cafés, workshops and meeting points **also nearby**. Tapping a row opens the place's full card in place; shared links, Near me and the walk open it the same way. Route progress counts stops ("0 / 20 stops visited"); Reset clears the whole route.
- **Open now and Step-free filters**: two toggles under the categories narrow the list and the map (and search matches on other routes) to places open right now, using the same status as the badge, special hours included, and to places the admin marked step-free. An open place's card stays even if it closes while being read. See `src/lib/place-filters.ts`.
- **Search across all routes**: searching shows matches on the current route as usual, plus an **Also on other routes** list (with each place's route) that switches to the route and opens the place. Matching forgives how names are typed: Arabic vowel marks, tatweel and letter variants (أ/إ/آ/ا, ة/ه, ى/ي), and English case, accents, hyphens and apostrophes ("rifai" finds "Al-Rifa'i"). It runs on the content in the app, so it works offline. See `src/lib/search.ts`.
- **Start the walk**: a full-screen guided mode for a route. It shows the current stop with its photo and audio guide, and the next stop with the live distance and a direction arrow (it turns with the phone's compass where available, otherwise shows "NE" and so on). Arriving at the next stop moves the walk there, marks it visited and offers its audio guide. Audio plays by itself only with **Play the guide when I arrive** (off by default, remembered on the device): then arriving at a stop starts its guide, once per stop, and never over a guide that's playing or paused. If the phone only allows audio after a tap (iOS), a **You've arrived. Tap to play the guide** button shows instead of failing silently. The screen stays on (Screen Wake Lock), and with a downloaded route it all works offline. **Show map** opens a small map (remembered on the device) with the route line, the current and next stops labelled, and your position and its accuracy; it keeps you and the next stop in view as you walk, until you pan it (**Follow me** resumes). Leaflet loads only when the map is first opened, and it uses the same OpenStreetMap tiles as the offline download. With location on, **Almost there: [stop]** shows about 60 m before the next stop, once per stop, with a gentle vibration where the phone supports it (never a sound), so a phone in a pocket doesn't walk past it; arrival then works as before. It can be turned off in the walk (remembered on the device). **Take a break** lists the three nearest cafés and restaurants and the nearest place with toilets (from visitor tips), on any route within about 1.5 km, with distance, walking time and open status, open ones first (from the visitor's position, or the current stop without one). Tapping one shows its card with **Back to the walk**, which returns to the same stop (Escape does too). See `src/components/WalkMode.tsx`, `src/components/WalkMiniMap.tsx` `src/lib/walk-mode.ts` and `src/lib/breaks.ts`.
- **Through the ages**: a place can have a build year (or range) and an era: Roman & Coptic, Early Islamic, Tulunid, Fatimid, Ayyubid, Mamluk, Ottoman, or Muhammad Ali & modern. It all works offline from the saved content, in every language; places without hours show nothing rather than a guess. See `src/lib/opening-hours.ts`.
- **Notices**: short announcements from the admin (a closure, building works, a crowded market day) show as a banner under the header, at the top of one route, or on one place's card and in walk mode, in the visitor's language, between optional first and last days (Cairo time). A visitor can close a notice (remembered on the device; an edited notice shows again); an important one, for safety, shows first and can't be closed. Part of the saved content, so it works offline. See `src/lib/notices.ts` and `src/components/Notices.tsx`.

On those dates the app uses them wherever it uses hours (the badge, the week table, the "likely closed when you get there" warnings and Plan my visit), tagged with the period's name ("Ramadan"); a closure reads "Closed today". ## Tech stack

- React 19, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS 4, Motion
- Leaflet / React Leaflet with OpenStreetMap tiles
- Google Gemini (`@google/genai`), called only from server-side functions
- `vite-plugin-pwa` / Workbox for offline support
- Hosted on Vercel (static frontend and functions in `api/`)

## Project structure

```
api/
  _lib/gemini.ts     Gemini calls (script, text-to-speech, live insights). Server only.
  audio-guide.ts     GET /api/audio-guide?id=<locationId>&lang=en|ar  → audio/wav
  live-info.ts       GET /api/live-info?id=<locationId>&lang=en|ar    → JSON
src/
  components/        UI: MainContent, MapView, AudioGuide, LiveLocationInfo, RouteDownload…
  data/locations.ts  Routes, categories and places (the content lives here)
  i18n/              Languages (lang.ts), interface strings (French, Spanish dictionaries) and RTL handling
```

The browser never receives the Gemini API key. It requests audio and insights by location ID, and the server builds the prompts from `src/data/locations.ts`. Generated audio is stored in Vercel Blob (keyed by the description text), so each place is generated once and survives deploys. Editing a description regenerates its audio automatically, and bumping `AUDIO_VERSION` in `api/_lib/gemini.ts` regenerates everything, for example after changing the voice or prompt.

### Keeping Gemini costs bounded

The API is public, so it limits how often Gemini can be called:

- **Strict queries.** The endpoints accept only `?id=<number>&lang=en|ar`. Any other parameter gets a `400`, so made-up URLs can't get around the CDN cache. Unknown places get a `404`. Neither ever calls Gemini.
- **Cached results.** Audio is generated once per place and language and kept in Blob, as described above. Live insights are kept in Blob for 24 hours (`LIVE_INFO_TTL_MS`), so each place and language costs at most one search-grounded Gemini call per day. Requests that arrive at the same time share a single call.
- **Failure cooldown.** If a Gemini call fails, repeat requests for that place get the same error for 60 seconds instead of retrying immediately.
- **Rate limit.** Each IP gets 40 API requests per minute per function instance, after which it receives a `429` with `Retry-After`. Responses served from the CDN cache don't count toward the limit. For a hard limit across all instances, also add a Vercel Firewall rate-limit rule on `/api/*`.

Because there are only a fixed number of places, there is a ceiling on Gemini usage. With a Blob store connected, it is roughly two calls per place and language for audio (whenever a description or `AUDIO_VERSION` changes) plus one live-insights call per place and language per day.


2. Create `.env.local` with your [Gemini API key](https://aistudio.google.com/apikey):
   ```bash
   GEMINI_API_KEY=your-key
   ```
3. Start the dev server at http://localhost:3000 (it also serves `/api/*`):
   ```bash
   npm run dev
   ```

Other scripts: `npm run build` (production build in `dist/`), `npm run preview`, `npm run lint` (type check), `npm test` (checks the routes and places in `src/data/locations.ts`), `npm run pregenerate-audio` (see [Pre-generating the audio guides](#pre-generating-the-audio-guides)), `npm run fetch-photos` (see [Photos](#photos)). `npm run check:bundle` (after a build; CI runs it) fails when the JavaScript loaded before the first paint passes its gzipped budget (215 KB; 191 KB now): load new code lazily rather than raising it.

Performance (Lighthouse, mobile, simulated slow 4G, on the production build): home 91, accessibility 100. The Google Fonts stylesheet doesn't block the first paint (it swaps in when loaded), and list thumbnails use 240px copies of the built-in photos (made with the 480px ones by `scripts/photo-variants.ts` and saved with offline routes).

Every pull request runs the type check, the tests and a production build on GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`).

## Deploying

The app deploys to Vercel from this repository. Set `GEMINI_API_KEY` in the Vercel project's environment variables. Only the functions in `api/` read it, and it is not included in the frontend build.

For persistent audio, create a **private** Blob store under the project's Storage tab and connect it to the project. This adds `BLOB_READ_WRITE_TOKEN`. Without it, the app still works, but audio is regenerated after each deploy.

### Pre-generating the audio guides

Audio is otherwise generated the first time a visitor plays a place, which makes that visitor wait. To generate every place in every language ahead of time:

```bash
vercel env pull .env.local
```

```bash
npm run pregenerate-audio
```

The first command copies `GEMINI_API_KEY` and `BLOB_READ_WRITE_TOKEN` from the Vercel project into `.env.local`. The script skips audio that's already stored for the current description and `AUDIO_VERSION`, so it's safe to run again at any time: after adding or editing places, after bumping `AUDIO_VERSION`, or to retry places that failed. It waits 2 seconds between Gemini calls to stay under rate limits.

Options: `-- --dry-run` lists what's missing without generating anything, `-- --lang=en` (or `ar`, `fr`, `es`) does one language, and `-- --delay=5000` changes the wait in milliseconds.

## Photos

Photos of the monuments and routes come from [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org) under CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0 or public-domain licenses. They're stored as resized WebP files in `public/images/photos/`, and each one's author, license and source are in `src/data/photos.ts`. The app credits each photo on the photo itself and in the **Photo credits** dialog at the bottom of the page. Places without a photo, mostly cafés, shops and workshops, keep the illustrated placeholder.

To add or change a photo, edit `scripts/photo-sources.ts`, then run:

```bash
npm run fetch-photos
```

Each entry names either an English Wikipedia article (the article's main image is used) or a specific Commons file. The script skips photos that are already downloaded (`-- --force` re-downloads them all; `-- --only=place-1` fetches one), refuses files that aren't freely licensed, and rewrites `src/data/photos.ts`. Commit the new images and that file. `npm test` checks that every photo exists and has an author and an allowed license.

Photos aren't part of the offline install. They're cached the first time they're viewed, or when a route is downloaded for offline use.

## Offline routes

"Download for offline" (`src/components/RouteDownload.tsx`) saves everything a route needs into the service worker's own caches, so they're served with no connection:

- **Audio guides** for every stop in the current language. The URL carries a hash of the description, so an edit in the admin makes the saved copy out of date and the route offers an update. Updates download only what changed.
- **Photos** of the route and its stops.
- **Map tiles**: the route area at zoom 14–16, plus zoom 17 just around each stop, capped at 400 tiles. OpenStreetMap's tile policy forbids bulk downloads of large areas at zoom 17 and above, so don't raise these limits.

What to download is worked out in `src/lib/offline-pack.ts`, and the download itself is in `src/lib/offline-store.ts`. The list of saved routes is kept in localStorage. The cache names are shared with `vite.config.ts` through `src/lib/offline-caches.ts`.

## Admin dashboard

`/admin` is a password-protected dashboard for managing the content without a code change:

- **Reports**: visitor reports, newest first. Apply a suggested map position in one click, delete a place reported as closed, or open it in the editor. Mark reports resolved or dismissed.
  Optionally, a **daily email** lists the reports that arrived since the last one (sent around 8 am Cairo time, and only on days with new reports). To turn it on:
  1. Create a free [Resend](https://resend.com) account and an API key.
  2. In Vercel, set `RESEND_API_KEY`, `ADMIN_EMAIL` (one address, or several separated by commas) and `CRON_SECRET` (any long random string), then redeploy.
  3. Use **Send test email** on the Reports tab to check it works.

  By default, emails come from Resend's shared test address, which can only send to the email you signed up to Resend with. To send to other addresses, verify your domain in Resend and set `DIGEST_FROM`, for example `Cairo Walks <reports@yourdomain.com>`. The job is `api/_lib/digest.ts`, scheduled in `vercel.json`.
- **Content**: what's still missing, with progress for each item across all places (photo, opening hours, French, Spanish) and the routes missing a language. The list of places can be filtered by item and by route, and **Edit** opens the place at the right section. Worked out in the browser from the content. See `src/admin/content-health.ts`.
- **Places**: edit, add or delete places, including moving them by dragging a pin, and upload a photo with its credit. **Preview** shows the place's open card as visitors will see it, built from the unsaved changes (photo and credit, name, era, open now, hours and fee, description with its glossary links, audio guide, visitor tips and Spot it), with a switch between English, Arabic (right to left), French and Spanish. Nothing is saved by previewing, and audio played in the admin isn't counted in the stats. The card's shared parts are in `src/components/PlaceDetails.tsx`, used by the app and the preview alike.
- **Photo descriptions**: under each place's and route's photo (uploaded or built in), **What the photo shows** has a short description in each language for screen readers, e.g. "The carved stone façade, with a shell-shaped niche above the door." **Draft with Gemini** looks at the photo and fills the empty ones for the admin to check (`api/_lib/describe-photo.ts`; only the app's own photo addresses, rate-limited). A new photo clears the old descriptions. The app uses the description in the visitor's language as the photo's alt text on cards, the map, walk mode, breaks and the finish screen, and the name where there's none. The Content checklist lists places whose photo has no description.
  Each place shows whether its **audio guide** is ready in each language for the saved description. After a save that changes a description, or adds a place, the new audio is generated straight away, so visitors never wait for it. Missing audio can also be generated with one tap. Audio made before transcripts were kept counts as missing, so generating it again (or **Generate missing**) adds the transcript. Generation is admin-only, skips audio that already exists, refuses text that hasn't been saved yet, and is capped at 30 per 10 minutes.
- **French and Spanish**: places, routes, glossary terms and special-hours names have optional fields in each. **Fill in missing French** / **Fill in missing Spanish** drafts the empty ones with Gemini: in a place, route, glossary term or special-hours editor it fills the form (only the fields still empty) for you to check and save; on the Places list it drafts every place missing that language and saves once, as one History entry you can restore. Nothing is translated on its own. See `api/_lib/translate.ts`.
- **Translations to review**: every field Gemini fills (names, descriptions, highlights, metro and getting-there notes, Spot it, photo descriptions, glossary, special-hours names, Help sheet items, notices) is marked as a draft on its item (`drafted: ['descriptionFr', …]`, saved with the content; visitors never see it). Each editor shows **Drafted by Gemini, not reviewed yet** with the fields and **Mark reviewed** (or **Mark all reviewed**); editing a field by hand counts as reviewed. The Content checklist lists **Translations to review** by language, with **Edit**. Marks of fields left empty are dropped. See `src/lib/drafted.ts` and `src/admin/DraftReview.tsx`.
- **Notices**: up to 10 announcements for visitors, each with text in English and Arabic (French and Spanish optional, with **Fill in missing**), where it shows (everywhere, a route or a place), optional first and last days, and an **Important** flag. Each shows whether it's showing now, starts later or has ended; the Content checklist lists ended ones to remove. A notice on a deleted route or place goes with it. See `src/admin/NoticesTab.tsx`.
- **Help**: the visitor Help sheet's own content: up to 5 local contacts (name in each language, phone), 5 extra tips under "Good to know" and 5 extra phrases (Arabic, with its meaning in each language). **Fill in missing French/Spanish** drafts the translations, marked to review like the rest. The emergency numbers stay fixed in the app. See `src/admin/HelpTab.tsx`.
- **Which language next**: the Content tab's French and Spanish tiles also show each language's share of visitors over the last 30 days ("0% translated · 14% of visitors", from the Stats counts), and a line names the language most worth translating next: the one whose visitors meet the most missing places. Without stats (no Blob store, offline) the tiles are as before.
- **Content checklist** also lists routes, glossary terms and special hours missing French or Spanish, each linking to its editor.
- **Routes**: edit, add or delete routes (the distance field shows the walk planned from the stops, e.g. "≈ 4.9 km", with **Use** to copy it in) and their photos (a route can only be deleted once it has no places), and set the stops' **walking order**.
  Each route also shows its **audio guides** per language ("9 of 13 ready"), with **Generate missing**, which makes the missing ones one place after another with progress and Cancel. At the server's limit (30 per 10 minutes) it stops and says how many it made, so running it again later carries on. See `src/admin/RouteAudio.tsx`.
- **Stats**: anonymous usage for the last 7, 30 or 90 days: audio plays, route opens and Near me arrivals, plays per day, **Languages** (the app's language for each route open and audio play, e.g. "English 61% · Arabic 22% · French 12% · Spanish 5%", with a note when part of the period predates it), the most-played audio guides, and for each route the plays at every stop in walking order (where visitors stop following it). Only daily totals are kept, in Blob (`stats/days/YYYY-MM-DD.json`): no cookies, visitor IDs or IP addresses. Each audio guide, route and arrival counts once per visit and is sent in one small batch; plays and route opens also carry the interface language (en, ar, fr or es, nothing else). Visitors with Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track on aren't counted. **Download (CSV)** saves the period shown as one spreadsheet (UTF-8, quoted cells) with a section per table: each day, languages, each place (plays, arrivals, helpful yes/no and the reasons), each route, and every route's stops in walking order (`src/admin/stats-csv.ts`). See `src/lib/stats.ts`, `src/lib/track.ts` and `api/_lib/stats.ts`.
- **History**: every save is kept, and any earlier version can be restored. **Download backup (JSON)** saves the whole content to keep outside the app, and **Download places (CSV)** a spreadsheet of the places (ID, route, kind, names and descriptions in each language, coordinates, whether hours are set, photo credit), UTF-8 with quoted cells so Excel and Google Sheets show the Arabic. **Restore from a backup file** checks the file like a save, shows what would change (places, routes, glossary terms and special hours added, removed or changed) and saves it as one History entry. See `src/admin/backup.ts`.

To turn it on, set `ADMIN_PASSWORD` (12+ characters) in the Vercel project's environment variables and connect a Blob store. Saves go live for visitors within about a minute, with no deploy.

How it works: the routes and places are stored as one JSON document in Blob (`content/current.json`). The app starts from the copy bundled in `src/data/locations.ts`, so it renders instantly and offline, then loads `/api/content`. The audio guide, live insights and `npm run pregenerate-audio` all read the live copy, so places added in the dashboard get audio too. Every save is validated on the server with the same rules as the tests (`src/data/content.ts`), and saves use the Blob ETag so two admins can't overwrite each other's changes. The dashboard is a separate bundle (`admin.html`), so none of its code is sent to visitors.

Uploaded photos are resized in the browser (960px wide, WebP) before upload, which also strips their metadata, including any GPS position a phone saved in them. They're stored in Blob and served from `/api/photo?id=<hash>` with year-long caching. An uploaded photo replaces the built-in one for that place or route, and removing it brings the built-in one back. Each needs a credit (who took it, and a license such as "Own photo" or "CC BY-SA 4.0"), shown on the photo and in **Photo credits**.

## Adding or editing places

Once the admin dashboard has saved, the live content is the copy in Blob, and edits should go through the dashboard. `src/data/locations.ts` is the starting content and the fallback. Every route, category and place is defined there. Each place needs an `id`, a `routeId`, English and Arabic names and descriptions, a `category` and coordinates. The audio guide and live insights work for new places automatically. Run `npm test` afterwards: it checks that every place has a valid route and category, text in both languages, and coordinates inside Cairo.
